Why the support/compliance work exists

requirements and source references

The references below describe underwriting and claims records, reasons for decisions, and delegated-authority requirements. Examiners may review underwriting notes, and claim files must support the determination. The applicable requirements depend on the jurisdiction and agreement.

The "underwriting file" is legally definedNAIC Ch.20: every input used to select, classify, rate, or terminate must be included in the file
Examiners read the underwriter's notesStandard 4 reviews "underwriter's file or notes on a system log"
Record reasons for declinationsvalid recorded reasons for every declination; agents' declinations monitored too
Adverse decisions need stated reasonsNAIC #670 "specific and accurate reasons"; FCRA adverse-action notices on credit-driven pricing
Document the reasons for claim decisionsStandard 5: documentation "sufficient to support or justify the ultimate claim determination"
Record claim datesloss · reported · sufficient-info · accepted/rejected — per claim
Identify the policy provisions behind a denialspecific policy provision + appeal instructions, mandated content
Lloyd’s delegated-authority categoriesFull → Pre-set Rates → No Discretion → Prior Submit (every risk referred)
Claims declination is rarely delegateda coverholder "will very rarely have authority to decline a claim" — refer denials to the authorized decision-maker
MGA oversight is statutoryNAIC #225: semi-annual on-site review of underwriting and claims operations
Fronting carriers review underwriting procedureshow risks are "selected, priced, referred, and declined" — diligence items
Bordereaux report premiums, risks, and claimspremium/risk/claims bordereaux + GPI tracking + aggregate reports by zone
When using AI: keep the information and reasons needed to review a decision. Check the relevant recordkeeping requirements before changing a workflow. Allianz’s announced work with Anthropic includes AI decision logging; see the company examples below.
